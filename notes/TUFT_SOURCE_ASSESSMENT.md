# Source Assessment: Topological Unified Field Theory

## Decision

The Nielsen manuscript is used here only as a **heuristic prompt** to ask
whether a global invariant can be preserved by a local reduction. None of its
gauge, gravity, particle-spectrum, mass, or experimental claims is used in a
definition, theorem, proof, simulation, or interpretation of the Kemeny or
Hex results.

The concrete response to that prompt is the Y-game majority reduction:
local three-cell majority operations preserve a rigorously defined global
connectivity winner. That statement comes from combinatorial game theory and
is independently executable.

## Why the boundary is necessary

The accessible PhilPapers record describes Jenny Lorraine Nielsen's
*The Topological Unified Field Theory on the Complex Hopf Fibration* as
forthcoming and makes sweeping unification and prediction claims. The linked
PDF returned HTTP 403 in the current research environment, so the complete
manuscript was not audited here.

A separate critical review by John Reimer Morales describes a coherent
classifying-space core but says the bridge from that mathematics to the
headline physical unification claim is not established by the current logic.
That is consistent with the repository's policy: interesting topology can
motivate a question, but physical conclusions require their own derivation
and independent validation.

## Admissible use in this repository

- **Allowed:** local-to-global invariants, recursive coarse-graining, and
  non-factorability as questions to formalize in finite combinatorial models.
- **Not allowed:** citing the manuscript as evidence that the Y theorem,
  Kemeny results, privacy claims, or market claims are true.
- **Required:** every transported idea must receive a new definition, proof
  or labeled computation within the target model.
