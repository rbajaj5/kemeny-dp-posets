# Tagore, "Useless" Work, and Research Discipline

## Source correction

The supplied Chinese transcript reads an untitled prose-poem about an idle
artist who is mistakenly admitted to a paradise organized entirely around
useful work. The artist paints a water pitcher and makes a colored hair ribbon;
these unnecessary objects interrupt the settlement's perfectly allocated
time.

The transcript locates the piece inside a Chinese Tagore anthology associated
with *Gitanjali*. The underlying English text is section 26 of
*The Fugitive-III* in Tagore's 1921 collection *The Fugitive*, not a section of
*Gitanjali*. It is also circulated under the editorial title "A Wrong Man in
Workers' Paradise." The original section is untitled.

The distinction matters because the repository treats bibliographic identity
as part of reproducibility. An insightful interpretation does not repair an
incorrect source label.

## What the story contributes

The story presents a society in which every unit of time already has a
recognized purpose. The artist introduces patterns, color, attention, and
choice without first supplying a utility argument. These changes create
irregularities in the schedule, but they also disclose preferences and forms
of life that the original accounting system could not represent.

For this repository, that is a philosophy of exploration, not a theorem:

- an investigation may begin before its application is known;
- an aesthetic, playful, or apparently irrelevant construction may expose a
  missing state variable or a badly specified objective;
- a negative result can be useful because it identifies a boundary; and
- a research program should not confuse visible busyness with progress.

The transcript's stronger suggestions - that the poem is Tagore's self-mockery
or that purposeless storytelling will be uniquely human after AI - are
interpretations. The text alone does not establish either claim.

## Exploration and certification are different phases

Permission to explore does not lower the publication standard. The repository
uses a two-phase rule.

### Exploration

- Follow an analogy, picture, simulation, or source even when its payoff is
  uncertain.
- Record surprising failures as well as successes.
- Allow a construction to remain interesting without forcing it into Kemeny,
  privacy, markets, or Hex.

### Certification

- Identify the source and version correctly.
- Define the target object and the map into the repository's mathematics.
- Supply a proof, counterexample, or reproducible computation.
- State the closest prior result and the remaining novelty uncertainty.
- Label the output `PROVED`, `KNOWN`, `COMPUTATIONAL`, `OPEN`, or
  `HEURISTIC`.

The painted pitcher belongs in exploration. A claim about what its pattern
preserves belongs in certification.

## Consequence for automated research

Automation can increase the amount of activity without increasing
understanding. The relevant objective is therefore not commits, simulations,
or tokens produced. A useful increment should do at least one of the
following:

1. establish a correct result;
2. falsify or narrow a proposed connection;
3. make an experiment reproducible;
4. improve provenance or error control; or
5. create a clearly labeled object whose value is exploratory rather than
   asserted.

This rule leaves room for work without an immediate purpose while preventing
the repository from converting every attractive pattern into a theorem.
