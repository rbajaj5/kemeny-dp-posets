# Three-Voter Applications of the Differential-Privacy Slides

## Status

This note maps the Peters three-voter boundary to the covering-relation,
smooth-sensitivity, Johnson-Lindenstrauss, sample-and-aggregate, and private
learning ideas in the supplied PDF. Results are generated by
`scripts/run_three_voter_applications.py`.

| Application | Status | What is supported |
|---|---|---|
| Hasse stability | **PROVED** | A unique non-unanimous three-voter metric median has cover radius one; a unanimous profile has radius three. |
| Subset-DP certificates | **PROVED + TESTED** | Distance-stratified subset DP returns exact score-gap and instability witnesses without factorial enumeration. |
| Smooth ranking bound | **PROVED** | The radius bound improves on global sensitivity only at unanimous three-voter profiles. |
| Exponential mechanism | **KNOWN + TESTED** | Exact finite-output distributions satisfy the DP inequalities on every three-candidate, three-voter profile and every neighbor. |
| Sample-and-center | **PROVED COMPONENTS + COMPUTATIONAL** | Exact two-ballot shortcut and sharp center factor-two lemma, plus utility comparisons; the pipeline is not itself private. |
| JL projection | **KNOWN THEOREM + COMPUTATIONAL** | Binary-coin and independent-column spherical projections satisfy Li's fixed-vector theorem; neither it nor the experiments imply privacy or Kemeny argmin preservation. |

## 1. Covering relations and Hasse shells

The slide deck represents one-record database changes as covers in a graded
poset. For ranking profiles, the cover graph is the multiset lattice. The exact
uniqueness radius is

```text
R(x) = min_tau ceil((C_x(tau) - C_x(sigma)) / d(sigma, tau)).
```

The new metric corollary says that a unique non-unanimous `n`-record median has
`R <= n-2`. Therefore three-voter profiles have a complete radius
classification:

- tied: radius `0`;
- unique and non-unanimous: radius `1`;
- unanimous: radius `3`.

Exact atlases verify this for every multiset of three rankings through five
candidates:

| Candidates | Profiles | Tied (`R=0`) | Unique non-unanimous (`R=1`) | Unanimous (`R=3`) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 56 | 2 | 48 | 6 |
| 4 | 2,600 | 272 | 2,304 | 24 |
| 5 | 295,240 | 54,640 | 240,480 | 120 |

The exhaustive counts are computational checks; the classification itself is
proved for every metric space.

The exact subset-DP implementation now also returns the full minimum-cost
profile indexed by Kendall distance from the selected optimum. Its radius and
constructive repeated-ballot witness match factorial enumeration on all 2,600
four-candidate, three-voter profiles. At the three-voter boundary the output
necessarily reproduces the radius dichotomy; larger profile sizes expose why
the distance denominator, rather than the second-best score alone, matters.

## 2. Smooth sensitivity

For the deterministic Kemeny selector, the current smooth upper bound is

```text
B_beta(x) = D exp(-beta max(R(x)-1, 0)).
```

At the three-voter boundary this becomes:

```text
unanimous profile:                  D exp(-2 beta)
tied or unique non-unanimous:       D
```

Thus distance to loss of uniqueness alone supplies no instance-specific
improvement on contested three-voter profiles. A better mechanism would need a
finer statistic, a different output geometry, or assumptions on the profile
domain.

## 3. Private learning and the exponential mechanism

Kemeny aggregation is empirical risk minimization over the finite hypothesis
class of transitive pairwise classifiers. Peters's result implies that exact
ERM remains NP-hard with three ranking records as the candidate count grows.
The exponential mechanism remains a valid information-theoretic private
learner, but enumerating its `m!` outputs is not scalable.

The repository now exposes the exact finite-output distribution and audits the
privacy likelihood-ratio inequalities on all three-candidate, three-voter
profiles and their add/remove neighbors.

For four candidates, averaging uniformly over the 2,600 multiset profiles
gives:

| Epsilon | Mean probability of an optimum | Mean additive cost regret |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.5 | 0.0599 | 4.2375 |
| 1.0 | 0.0707 | 3.9507 |
| 2.0 | 0.0956 | 3.4080 |
| 4.0 | 0.1563 | 2.5141 |

These are exact finite-profile averages, not population PAC guarantees.

## 4. Sample-and-aggregate

The block experiment uses 60 Mallows ballots over four candidates, exact block
Kemeny estimates, and the Nissim-Raskhodnikova-Smith center of attention.
Block size two lies below the Peters boundary; exact block size three reaches
the worst-case hardness boundary.

Under moderate concentration (`phi=0.5`), block sizes two and three matched
the full Kemeny ranking in 97 and 99 of 100 trials. Under weaker concentration
(`phi=0.8`), both match rates were 52%. The experiment
does not show a utility discontinuity at block size three; Peters's result is a
worst-case computational obstruction, not a distributional utility lower
bound.

At `phi=1`, deterministic tie-breaking creates a visible identity-ranking
bias. Those rows should not be interpreted as economic evidence. The exact
audit proves that the minimizing-center set is relabeling equivariant while
the lexicographic selector is not; a set-valued or randomized resolution is
required for neutrality.

The separate sample-and-center audit checks the sharp metric factor two on
8,581 center instances, exact two-ballot output on 612 ordered pairs, and
Borda utility on 3,002 profiles. See `notes/SAMPLE_AGGREGATE_AUDIT.md`.

## 5. Johnson-Lindenstrauss geometry

The pairwise sign embedding satisfies the exact identity

```text
||v_sigma - v_tau||_2^2 = 4 d_K(sigma, tau).
```

The experiment applies shared dense Rademacher projections to all 120 rankings
on five candidates and asks whether projected least-squares Kemeny retains the
original optimum. It includes every 120 unanimous profile and 400 additional
random three-voter profiles.

| Projected dimension | Containment, radius 1 | Containment, radius 3 |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.316 | 1.000 |
| 8 | 0.318 | 1.000 |
| 16 | 0.439 | 1.000 |
| 32 | 0.605 | 1.000 |

Unanimous optima always remain among projected optima because their projected
cost is zero. Non-unanimous optima are much less stable. Every profile-level
relative-distortion margin certificate was respected in the experiment, but
many profiles were not certifiable at the tested dimensions.

This confirms a limitation of the JL idea: preserving pairwise geometry
approximately does not automatically preserve a small-gap Kemeny argmin, and
the projection does not remove the need to optimize over transitive rankings.

The matched construction audit adds Li's independent-column spherical
projection beside the binary-coin construction. For all 120 rankings on five
candidates, Li's explicit sufficient dimension at `epsilon=0.4` and
`delta=0.05` is 1,476 for one fixed difference vector and 5,025 after a union
bound over all 7,140 unordered ranking pairs. Both exceed the exact ambient
dimension 10, so the theorem does not certify compression on this complete
small family.

In 16 trials per dimension, the mean fractions of all ranking pairs within
the `0.4` distortion target were:

| Projected dimension | Binary coin | Spherical columns |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.329 | 0.326 |
| 8 | 0.659 | 0.633 |
| 16 | 0.827 | 0.831 |
| 32 | 0.938 | 0.919 |
| 64 | 0.993 | 0.988 |

Dimensions above 10 illustrate concentration rather than reduction. The
source theorem, implementation details, fixed-vector experiment, and privacy
boundary are recorded in
[`JL_CONSTRUCTION_ASSESSMENT.md`](JL_CONSTRUCTION_ASSESSMENT.md).
